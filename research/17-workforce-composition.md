# Workforce Composition — Gender in Indonesian Tech + Fresh-Grad Entry Conditions

Research date: 2026-11-09. Scope: WHO is in the IT workforce (gender) + HOW newcomers enter (fresh-grad placement/first-job conditions). Companion to prior findings (SMK absorption, MSIB/DT S numbers, two-tier market verdict — not repeated).

## Headline Numbers

- **Women are ~27–28% of Indonesia's ICT/tech workforce** (ILO–BPS Sakernas 2019: 28.0% women; McKinsey 2020: 27%; BCG 2020: 22%) vs **~40% of the total national workforce** — tech is more male than the economy at large.
- **Production side is the bottleneck start**: only **27.88% of Engineering-field (Teknik) students are women** (Statistik Pendidikan Tinggi 2025, TA 2024/25, N=1,619,754); at ITB's Teknik Informatika program just **18.4% are women** (all-time records). But IPB's Ilmu Komputer is **54.9% women** — the pipeline is institutionally heterogeneous, not uniformly male.
- **Gender pay gap among Indonesian professionals/technicians ≈ 33%** (ILO-modeled, 2025); cross-country developer-survey research shows the gap concentrated at the *bottom* of the wage distribution (sticky floor) in lower-middle-income economies — hardest on exactly the fresh grads in scope.
- **Top-campus placement is fast, salaries solid**: UI Fasilkom 2024 grads median **Rp9M/month**; 81.2% of UI grads employed within 6 months (median wait 2 months); UGM 91.6% within 6 months; ITB mean wait 2.11 months. — BUT these are survey results of elite campuses with 53–89% response rates, not the national picture.
- **Entry-level oversupply is quantifiable at the platform level**: Jobstreet reports **200 (2023) → 400+ (2026) → 500–600 (Mar 2026) applicants per posting**; MagangHub 2026: **300,000 applicants for 50,000 slots (6:1)**; two-thirds of large-company postings are entry-level yet each draws hundreds of applications.

## Methodology Assessment

1. **Production-side gender is the strongest data we have** — PDDikti full census via the official statistical yearbook (near-universal HEI reporting via neo-feeder). Main limitation: 2025 book gives gender by *bidang* (Ten fields), not by prodi, so the 27.88% Teknik figure includes civil/mechanical/chemical engineering. Prodi-level evidence is patchwork (ITB dashboard 18.4% F; IPB report 54.9% F) and shows how misleading the aggregate could be; a real TI/SI national gender figure requires the PDDikti prodi dataset (data.kemdiktisaintek XLSX exports) — flagged as follow-up.
2. **Workforce-side gender rests on four independent-but-old estimates converging on 27–28%** (ILO/BPS 2019, McKinsey 2020, BCG 2020, ILO 2020). All predate 2021; no post-2021 national ICT gender measurement found. Company disclosures (GoTo 33.5%) are recent and whole-workforce but subject to image management, and GoTo is a tech *company*, not the sector.
3. **University tracers are the best placement evidence but two caveats**: (a) they cover elite publics (UI/ITB/UGM/IPB) and one elite private (BINUS) — top-campus grads are the most placeable slice of the TI cohort, so these numbers are an *upper bound* on fresh-grad placement; (b) response rates 53–89% and salary self-reporting invite both nonresponse and inflation bias. BINUS figures are explicitly promotional (Conf 2).
4. **Oversupply signals are platform executive statements**, consistent in direction across 2021→2026 (200→400-600 applicants/posting) but not IT-specific and not independently auditable. The MagangHub 6:1 ratio is the best government-counted number (registration counts, not survey).
5. **Bootcamp outcomes are usable only for Hacktiv8**, which publishes CIRR-format methodology with denominators; its "in-field" rate conflates full-time with internship/contract (33% of placements), which materially changes the picture for "getting a real job." All other bootcamps' claims lack denominators.
6. BPS avoided except where methodology was described by ILO (the 28% figure) — compliant with method rules.

## Data Gaps

1. **National prodi-level gender split for TI/SI** — Statistik Dikti 2025 only breaks gender by bidang; the raw PDDikti per-prodi dataset (XLSX exports on data.kemdiktisaintek.go.id) likely contains it but was not parsed in this pass. Highest-value follow-up.
2. **Multi-year trend of female share in informatics prodi** — nothing public found.
3. **Post-2021 national ICT gender share** — all 27–28% estimates predate 2021; no repeating BPS/ILO tech-gender series identified.
4. **% of Indonesian IT workers from non-CS backgrounds** — no survey with N>300 found; bootcamps don't publish intake composition. Best available proxies are qualitative.
5. **Median age of Indonesian developers** — no published survey slice; only GoTo's company-level age table and LinkedIn membership shares.
6. **IT-specific applicants-per-posting counts** — Jobstreet figures are all-industry.
7. **IT-specific gender pay gap in Indonesia** — only all-professionals Statista/ILO and income-group developer study exist.
8. **BINUS 6-month placement rate with clean methodology** — only at-graduation employment shares published; Tracer Agent program exists but 2021+ results not public.
